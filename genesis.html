The Origin

Story Time !

Nike? No, Blue Ribbon

The story starts in 1964 at the University of Oregon. Phil Knight, a talented track runner, went to Stanford Business School, where he wrote a paper arguing that high-quality, affordable Japanese running shoes could challenge the dominant German brands (should I say their names here 👀? Well, Adidas and Puma).

In 1964, Knight traveled to Japan and convinced the makers of Onitsuka Tiger shoes (now known as Asics) to let him distribute their shoes in the US. He shook hands with Coach Bowerman, they both put up $500 each, and

Blue Ribbon Sports (BRS)
was born.

New Tech!

Bill Bowerman was obssesed about making lighter shoes with better grip. The tension between BRS and Onitsuka Tiger shoes were rising. That's not good for the affairs.

A morning in 1971, Bill had an illumination while staring at the waffle iron of his wife. "What if I poured liquid urethane into the waffle iron ?". He did, and created a lightweight, grooved rubber sole that revolutionized running traction. That sounds interesting!

When does Nike comes in ?

BRS needed a brand new name for their new shoes. Phil wanted to call the company "Dimension Six". Perhaps it sounded futuristic and innovative for him. The employee team absolutely hated the name (and we can understand them). They even had a joke that it sounded more like a sci-fi spaceship.

Their very first employee, Jeff Johnson , had a dream about Nike, the Greek winged goddess of victory. The name stuck.

Fun Fact: There is a common legend that says that Phil Knight payed $35 to a random graphic design student to draw the iconic swoosh. That's correct, but the legend forget to say that Caroline Davison was later given a massive block of Nike stocks as a compensation: worth +$1.2M today!
